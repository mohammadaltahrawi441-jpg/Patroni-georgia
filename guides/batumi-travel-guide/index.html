Batumi: what to do and when to go

The boulevard, the botanical garden, Ali & Nino, Gonio fortress and the beaches south — plus an honest account of Batumi outside the summer season.

What Batumi actually is

A Black Sea port with a nineteenth-century old town, a six-kilometre seafront boulevard, and a skyline of new towers that looks like nowhere else in the Caucasus. It is Georgia's summer capital and its most international city — you will hear Turkish, Russian, Arabic and Georgian on the same street.

Two things to set expectations. The beach is pebble, not sand, and the water is clean and deep close in. And the city is emphatically seasonal: the Batumi of August and the Batumi of February are different places.

The things worth your time

  • The Seaside Boulevard. Six kilometres of promenade, cycle lanes, cafés and the dancing fountains. Walk it at dusk; this is what people actually remember about Batumi.
  • The Botanical Garden. Nine kilometres north on a headland above the sea, with plant collections from every continent laid out across a hillside. Give it half a day — it is much bigger than people expect and it is the best thing in the city.
  • Ali & Nino. Two seven-metre steel figures on the waterfront that move slowly through each other every evening. Free, brief and genuinely worth standing still for.
  • The Old Town and Europe Square. Nineteenth-century facades, the astronomical clock, and the cafés that stay open out of season.
  • The Dolphinarium, the cable car up Anuria Hill, and the Piazza.

South to Gonio and Kvariati

Twelve to eighteen kilometres south, twenty-five minutes by taxi, and a different coast: clearer water, quieter beaches, and mountains rising directly behind them.

Gonio-Apsaros Fortress is a Roman fort a few hundred metres from the sea, with walls and towers still standing and a quiet site inside. It is one of the oldest things you can walk around in Georgia and it is almost never crowded.

Kvariati bay is the better swimming. If Batumi's beach feels too busy, this is twenty-five minutes and a different holiday.

The Turkish border at Sarpi is a few kilometres further on.

When to go, honestly

July and August are the season: hot, full, expensive, and the city at maximum energy. Book ahead.

June and September are the sweet spot — the sea is warm, the boulevard is alive, the prices are lower and you can get a table.

May and October are pleasant for the city and marginal for swimming.

November to March is wet. Batumi gets serious rainfall, a good deal of the seafront trade closes, and the city turns back into a working Georgian port. It is cheap, atmospheric and quiet, and if you came for a beach holiday it will be a disappointment. It is a good time to see the Old Town and a bad time to book a sea-view apartment.

Common questions

Does Batumi have a sandy beach?

No — the beaches here are pebble. The water is clean and drops away quickly close to shore. Beach shoes are worth packing, and Kvariati south of the city has the clearer water.

How far is Batumi from Tbilisi?

Roughly 370 km, five to six hours by road or a comfortable train journey. There are also short domestic flights in season.

Is Batumi worth visiting in winter?

For the Old Town, the food and the prices, yes. For the beach, no — it is wet, much of the seafront closes and the sea is not swimmable. Go in June or September if the coast is your reason for coming.
